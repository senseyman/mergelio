# Fixed

- A slow pre-commit hook, or a signed commit waiting for your GPG passphrase, was stopped after 30 seconds. Commit, reword, merge, and continuing a cherry-pick or revert now wait as long as they need, and the busy bar has a **Cancel** button for a hook that is stuck.
- Cancelling a merge while its hook was running could leave the merged files staged as if they were your own changes. They are now unstaged. Other edits in the working tree are kept.
- Skipping a commit during a rebase, cherry-pick or revert, and running a signed rebase, could be stopped after 30 seconds. They now get the same longer limit as the rest of the operation, and can be cancelled.
- Cancelling a rebase while it was signing a commit left that commit staged with no way to continue, and skipping would drop it. Continuing now applies it again and keeps any conflict resolution you just made. Cancelling during a squash or fixup aborts the rebase.
- In repositories that use Git LFS, checking out a branch or a remote branch, and the switch before a drag-and-drop merge or rebase, could time out while LFS files were downloading. They now have a longer limit and a **Cancel** button. Stashing gets the longer limit too.
- Cancelling a checkout partway could leave the working tree half switched. Files it had already written are now put back. A cancel that comes after the checkout has finished, while the post-checkout hook runs, is reported as done and can be undone, and a merge or rebase that was to follow does not start.
- Cancelling an operation, or one that timed out, could leave `.git/index.lock` behind, so every later change failed until the file was deleted by hand. Git now gets the chance to clean up first.
- **Cancel** stops git itself. Programs that a hook or filter started still run to the end.
